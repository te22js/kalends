# Pompeii: the smallest useful external playtest

**Prospective protocol, 21 October 2026. No external playtest has been run.**
There are no invented participant quotes, enjoyment scores or retention results.
This protocol tests whether the playable slice is understandable and worth
continuing; it does not validate historical learning, cognitive acceleration or
human-like NPC psychology.

## Two stages, modest claims

Recruit **6 adult formative participants**, then **12 different adult players**
for an uncoached product test. Include people with and without simulation-game
experience and at least four who did not arrive because they follow AI research.
Exclude team members and anyone already trained on the demonstration. This is
a convenience sample for large usability failures, not a representative market
or a powered trial of small effects. Disclose recruitment and prior experience.

Use the formative six to find blockers, then revise the build freely. Freeze
build/configuration, two scenario seeds, task wording, metrics and exclusions
before the independent twelve. Do not tune on those twelve and describe the
result as untouched validation. Pay a fixed amount for initial study time,
independent of praise or task success; explain logging and voluntary withdrawal.

The tester receives the ordinary launch/Play instructions and opening experience,
without research documentation or a tour of World Lab. Use a fresh save and
ordinary player mode. Record installation/launch failures as onboarding failures,
not excluded users. A staff rescue counts as assistance. Do not recruit only
people who already know how the engine works.

## Session: 25 minutes plus optional play

1. **Enter unaided.** Say: “Try this historical world. Find out who you are and
   decide what to do.” Observe navigation, initial objective comprehension and
   the first meaningful action. A meaningful action changes resources,
   information, an obligation, a relationship or an actual investigation;
   walking or opening panels alone does not qualify.
2. **Choose with stakes.** Once they encounter a real objective, ask them to
   describe two feasible approaches, who benefits and what each might cost.
   Do not reveal the intended best route; if only one viable route exists, record
   a design failure rather than coaching a second one into existence.
3. **Commit and notice.** Let them act and wait/advance time. Ask what changed
   before highlighting a relationship or journal entry. Verify each claimed
   consequence against actual state. A branch or communication may have no
   effect; truthful nulls are acceptable.
4. **Question a character.** Ask them to investigate one actual consequence and
   open the explanation/evidence controls available in player mode. Score four
   questions: what was said/done, what the character could know, what executed
   outcome followed, and whether any alternative was actually tested. Earlier
   events and structural links alone must not earn “proved causation” credit.
5. **Try Lived Time.** Have them inspect a short LIVE interaction and then use
   an accelerated return/MONTAGE/SKIP control supported by the build. Ask which
   obligations/relationships changed, what the montage omitted and what could
   interrupt the advance. These must be actual recorded changes. Do not invent
   days of autonomous hosted activity while the application was closed.
6. **Choose to continue.** End the required task and offer up to ten additional
   unpaid-choice minutes within the compensated appointment: “You can stop now,
   or keep playing.” Compensation is unchanged. Record active continuation,
   voluntary new actions and the reason for stopping, without persuading them.

Counterbalance the order of LIVE and accelerated demonstrations across the
twelve and the two frozen scenarios. This reduces a simple novelty/order bias;
it does not make the small convenience sample a definitive causal experiment.
If comparing modes quantitatively, keep initial scenario and targets matched
through cloned saves, while avoiding a second run that simply gives away the
solution. Label any unmatched comparison exploratory.

## Primary observations and prospective internal gates

These are product decisions, not validated industry benchmarks. Report counts
and intervals, not just “passed.” The small study can tell us to fix the game;
passing it is not proof of product–market fit.

| Measure | Proposed useful signal among 12 independent testers | Failure implication |
|---|---|---|
| Launch/onboarding | At least10 launch and move/interact without live staff instructions | Simplify install/opening controls before recruiting more. |
| First meaningful action | At least9 act within5minutes of first playable screen, unaided | Opening lacks understandable agency. Report median/range and all failures. |
| Choice comprehension | At least9 describe two actual options and one real tradeoff before acting | The advertised dilemma is not legible or not real. |
| Consequence noticed | At least8 identify a genuine change without the facilitator pointing to it | State changes exist but are not experientially communicated. |
| Evidence comprehension | At least9 score3/4; count unsupported causal claims separately | Explanation UX or grounding needs revision. |
| Enjoyment | At least8 rate enjoyment≥5/7, separately from technical interest | Admiration for the engine is not enough. |
| Voluntary continuation | At least6 continue≥5 active minutes and perform a new meaningful action | Short demo appeal has not translated into continued play. |
| Accelerated return | At least9 identify two actual important changes after the return; at least8 prefer having this pacing option | Compression/summary may remove understanding or simply feel like skipping content. |
| Historical boundary | At least9 correctly classify an attested place, a reconstruction and an invented event | Source labels are insufficient for educational positioning. |

Ask separate seven-point ratings for enjoyment, clarity, agency, character
distinctiveness, interest in the technology and desire to return. Ask for one
unprompted memorable event and one boring/confusing moment. Do not ask leading
questions such as whether the “novel causal engine” was impressive. A character
believability question should refer to a witnessed decision and perceived goal,
not whether the character is conscious or has genuine feelings.

For four evidence questions and the history classification task, prepare an
answer key from the actual world/ledger and the source ledger. Two reviewers
score deidentified answers independently; record disagreement and reconcile it.
An inaccessible event must not appear in the player answer key as if it were
available evidence. Keep World Lab separate until the required player test ends.

## Instrumentation and complete-session cost

Capture consented pseudonymous participant/session IDs; build/config hashes;
seed; device/browser; launch/error timestamps; player position/location changes;
interaction/action proposals and confirmations; rejects/clarifications; canonical
event IDs; journal/evidence views; time-mode transitions; clock advancement;
save/restart outcomes; and active play intervals. Record duration of assistance,
not only successes. Log typed dialogue only with specific consent; redact or
delete personal content, and avoid unrelated screen/keystroke collection.

For a full session measure client frame time/FPS, input-to-visible-response
p50/p95, server simulation CPU and wall time, processed events/candidate checks,
peak RAM, network bytes, journal/snapshot growth and failures. Record reference
hardware and population/horizon/branches. Proposed usability budgets are p95
ordinary-action response under250ms and sustained30FPS on a declared reference
desktop; these are targets, not measured performance. Longer advances should
show progress/cancel behavior and disclose actual simulated time.

For optional providers, log actual model/version, tokens, retries, validation
failures, timeout/fallback, latency and receipt-priced cost. The offline route
has no provider inference bill; machine time is not free. Do not infer hosted
unit cost from a local CPU timing or use an assumed tariff example as an actual
receipt. Content authoring, historical review, art, support and moderation belong
in commercial economics alongside runtime cost.

Timing games and felt duration are not evidence of cognitive acceleration.
Simulation-fidelity/runtime experiments belong in the separate frozen technical
study; participants' satisfaction with a montage does not prove the skipped
computation preserved every necessary transition.

## Return, expansion and analysis

Invite voluntary return to the same save; do not schedule a paid return task and
call it retention. Report whether access remains possible, save integrity and
unprompted resumed meaningful activity within days2–8. With twelve people this
is qualitative follow-up, not a stable retention estimate. Stated intent and
observed return are separate.

If the initial gates hold, run a **new 60-adult return pilot** with initial pay
only, no return reminders/rewards, and a fixed seven-day window. A prospective
business signal is at least15/60 returning for≥10 active minutes and a meaningful
action, with a Wilson95% lower bound above15%. This is an internal threshold,
not an industry norm or confirmed product–market fit. Intermediate uncertainty
requires a fresh larger cohort; do not quietly change the denominator or window.

For authoring, a later **five-creator pilot** should require a new institution,
objective or interaction, not renamed residents. Measure time, staff help,
validation failures and engine edits; at least3/5 should complete a different
valid small scenario within one working day without an engine change. Then have
players try their output. Exporting a file is not proof of a usable platform.

Analyze all attempted sessions. Treat crashes and staff rescues as failures for
the affected task, reporting their causes separately. Use participant-level
intervals; clicks, NPCs, ticks and repeated actions are not independent human
samples. Report order/scenario differences descriptively. Label all post-result
subgroups exploratory. No significance claim is appropriate from twelve players.

Stop/revise if saves corrupt, player actions mislead, explanations invent
history, accessibility blocks participation or a participant wants to stop.
No school, clinical or professional efficacy claim follows from this adult UX
pilot. A separate expert-reviewed, adequately designed learning/transfer study
would be required for those uses.

The practical decision is simple: repair comprehension and consequence legibility
before broadening the world. Expand only when people choose more play and return,
and when a complete session has measured, sustainable cost. **No planned study
in this document is an observed result.**

# Applications: evidence gates, not a combined market

**21 October 2026.** The core research is a persistent causal and epistemic
world: authoritative events, bounded views, consequences, and player-controlled
pacing. Pompeii is the first product experiment. The seven verticals below have
different users, validation and operations; they are not seven established
products, and their hypothetical revenues must not be summed as a TAM.

This is a prospective portfolio decision document. The sprint handoff and actual
tests define delivered features. No playtest, learning transfer, professional
competence, human behavioral realism, neurological benefit or neural-interface
capability is claimed here.

## Need, available technology and required evidence

| Vertical | User need and first plausible customer | Available substrate / real alternatives | Evidence that would justify expansion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Experiential entertainment** | Adult players want agency, attachment and a reason to return to a historical or fictional life. First customer: history/systemic-game enthusiast. | Conventional game engines, authored narrative and local simulation already support many primitives; Sims, RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress compete for this leisure time. Optional language adds expression, not guaranteed depth. | Uncoached users perform meaningful actions, enjoy the consequences, voluntarily continue and return; actual paid conversion supports content economics. A beautiful recording or agent count is insufficient. |
| **2. Education** | Learners/teachers need to reason about historical evidence, institutions and causal tradeoffs. First pilot buyer: an adult-learning provider or museum with a defined lesson. | Source-labelled scenarios, structured debriefs and ordinary desktop interaction are tractable. Existing teaching materials, games and guided tours are inexpensive substitutes. | Independent historian review; preregistered comparison against equal-time instruction; delayed factual/source-memory retention and novel transfer tasks, not just presence or enjoyment. Track fictional details misremembered as history. |
| **3. Professional training** | Organizations need safe practice in communication, coordination and decision-making. First candidate: a low-stakes team-training provider with an explicit rubric. | Role simulation, controlled scenarios and observable executed outcomes; competes with facilitators and existing simulation/character tools such as Inworld/Convai. | Domain experts validate scenarios, scoring and feedback; blinded assessment on new real tasks or standardized roleplays. Demonstrate transfer and error recovery before any consequential certification use. Pompeii itself does not validate modern professional practice. |
| **4. Social science/research** | Researchers need controlled mechanisms, interventions and reproducible information access. First candidate: a lab with a specific experiment, not a general society oracle. | Seeded worlds, provenance and replay; AgentSociety and ordinary agent-based/DES frameworks are close substitutes. | Reproducible configurations, isolated ablations, matched information/costs, defensible independent units and comparison to observed target data when making real-world claims. Internal consistency alone is not empirical social validity. |
| **5. Creator platform** | Authors want to make coherent worlds without core programming and reach interested players. | Private scenario packages, validation, debugging and writer tools are plausible; Roblox, Sims creation/Gallery and ink already serve creators. | At least five outside authors create meaningfully different scenarios without engine edits, at measured time/support cost; their worlds attract voluntary use. Sharing files is not evidence of a two-sided network. |
| **6. Adaptive cognitive environments** | A speculative wish to learn more effectively from structured experience; the relevant customer and willingness to pay remain unknown. | Adaptive instruction, pacing, replay and structured practice are feasible research components. DES clock advances do not produce additional conscious years or speed biological memory consolidation. | Human randomized studies with equal real time, delayed retention and out-of-world transfer; source monitoring, workload and adverse-experience measures. Felt duration is a separate exploratory outcome. No cognitive-acceleration marketing before this evidence. |
| **7. Future immersive interfaces** | Some experiences may benefit from embodied spatial/social presence or accessible alternative controls. | VR hardware and conventional input interfaces exist, but this browser slice is not a validated VR product. Neural-linked experience delivery is a much more distant dependency, not a present platform feature. | For VR, compare the same content/control task on desktop and headset with usability, learning, comfort and accessibility outcomes. Neural directions require independent specialist science, ethics and any applicable clinical evidence; a world-engine test cannot establish them. |

## Operations, defensibility and feasibility

The following cost drivers are design hypotheses, not measured vendor invoices.
Optional token/voice examples and all business assumptions appear in
[COMPANY_THESIS.md](COMPANY_THESIS.md).

| Vertical | Cost and distribution | Potential moat / strongest objection | Ethics and near-term decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entertainment | Authoring/art/QA, optional inference, acquisition and save support; browser demo, interested communities, then an appropriate game storefront | Distinct content and retained player attachment; ordinary engines can copy individual features | Truthful history/fiction labels, respectful representation and privacy. **First priority**, conditional on actual enjoyment. |
| Education | Curriculum mapping, expert review, localization, teacher support and procurement; museum/educator pilots | Trusted content and validated lessons; purchasing is fragmented and free material competes strongly | Avoid confident historical falsehoods, grading by an unvalidated model and unconsented child telemetry. **Small adult pilot after review**, not a school-platform launch. |
| Training | Expert scenario/scoring work, facilitation, secure deployment and integration; direct specialist sales | Verified transfer and institutional integration; bespoke services can consume margin | No high-stakes competency inference from game scores; confidential workplace data requires clear control. **Discovery only** until a domain partner and validation exist. |
| Research | Reproduction support, experiment engineering, compute and maintenance; public artifacts and specific lab collaborations | Useful controlled fixtures; open substitutes and tiny support budgets | Do not present synthetic agents as human subjects or causal effects as policy prescriptions. **Narrow tool/service fallback**, separate commercial test. |
| Creator platform | Authoring UX, compatibility, hosting, discovery, revenue share, abuse handling and moderation; creators need players, not only tooling | Productive creator/player loop if observed; multihoming and incumbent networks weaken lock-in | IP, historical misrepresentation, personal data and public-world abuse. **Private authoring only** until evidence and operations justify public UGC. |
| Cognitive environments | Study design/recruitment, instructional expertise, longitudinal follow-up and privacy; distribution through validated educational use, not neurological promises | Validated transfer and safe instructional design; novelty/presence can masquerade as learning | Consent, source confusion, overstimulation, manipulation and accurate claims. **Research horizon**, no efficacy or medical product claim. |
| Immersive interfaces | Hardware access, 3D/UI production, comfort testing and device QA; headset ecosystems or accessibility channels | Task-specific embodiment benefit; device vendors own distribution and commodity engines support XR | Cybersickness, accessibility, exposure control and biometric privacy. **Optional VR experiment later**; neural interfaces remain speculative and outside current deliverables. |

## Shared engine investments and what remains separate

Invest first in the common requirements that the current world actually needs:
authoritative transitions; explicit private evidence; durable identities/accounts;
commitments and relations; interruption-aware clocks; versioned saves and world
packages; deterministic fallbacks; validated model proposals; and factual
presentation with inspectable support. Temporal compression needs a supported
fidelity contract, not only an accelerated animation.

Keep application-specific layers separate: entertainment direction, historical
claims, lesson objectives, professional competency rubrics, model calibration,
creator governance and immersive input. A generic “utility” score cannot stand
in for all seven. Report delivery, economic assumptions, player enjoyment and
educational transfer in their own units. The prior negative lineage result is
evidence against assuming an epistemically appealing rule improves every outcome.

Long histories create storage/versioning and bounded-recall problems even if
background simulation becomes faster. Multiplayer adds authority synchronization,
identity, malicious participants and moderation. These costs do not disappear
because a single local deterministic town works. Preserve replaceable language
providers and an inspectable offline route so a vendor price/availability change
does not destroy the core experience.

## Evidence sequence and stopping rules

**Now:** finish one playable district and honest temporal comparison; externally
review its history; run the small protocol in
[PLAYTEST_PROTOCOL.md](PLAYTEST_PROTOCOL.md). Fix navigation and weak choices
before expanding geography or model calls.

**After usable play:** run an independent return pilot and real opt-in price
test. If people admire the demo but do not continue or return, narrow the
entertainment proposition. A research tool may still be useful but is a
different business, not evidence the consumer thesis succeeded.

**After player value:** test private outside authoring and one educational lesson.
Stop creator-platform expansion if staff must edit the engine for every scenario.
Stop educational efficacy claims if gains disappear on delayed/transfer tests.
Only then consider hosted sharing, more worlds or a task-specific VR interface.

Cognitive/neural aspirations should not drive near-term budgets or valuation.
They are research questions, not required dependencies for a useful game. Failure
to show biological acceleration is not a defect in a project that accurately
offers entertainment or supported instruction.

## Evidence guardrails and sources

- [Official Pompeii evidence](HISTORICAL_SOURCE_LEDGER.md) supports selected
  historical constraints, not calibrated ancient economic behavior.
- [Makransky, Terkildsen & Mayer (2019; online2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321978607_Adding_Immersive_Virtual_Reality_to_a_Science_Lab_Simulation_Causes_More_Presence_But_Less_Learning)
  experimentally separates presence from learning in one science-lab setup;
  neither universal VR harm nor Kalends efficacy follows.
- [Mullen & Davidenko (2021), publisher abstract](https://brill.com/view/journals/time/9/4/article-p377_377.pdf)
  concerns prospective duration judgments and order effects. Direct full-text
  access failed in this session; no numerical extrapolation is made here.
- [Windrum, Fagiolo & Moneta (2007)](https://www.jasss.org/10/2/8.html)
  distinguishes internal model processes from empirical validation.
- [Current Roblox creation](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/02/accelerating-creation-powered-roblox-cube-foundation-model),
  [EA Sims](https://www.ea.com/games/the-sims), [ink](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/),
  [AgentSociety](https://github.com/tsinghua-fib-lab/AgentSociety),
  [Inworld](https://inworld.ai/) and [Convai](https://convai.com/) are primary
  competitor sources checked21October2026, not comparative effectiveness studies.

The [novelty audit](NOVELTY_AUDIT.md) records technical prior art and reading
limits. No source establishes that a faster simulated clock creates faster
human cognition or longer lived subjective experience.

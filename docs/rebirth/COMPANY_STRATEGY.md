# Kalends after Rebirth: earn a return before building a platform

21 October 2026. Product claims below refer to the implemented and executed local game paths, not a proposed universal world engine. Browser presentation evidence belongs in `VISUAL_QA.md` and `demo/rebirth/`. No adoption, revenue, external playtest or retention evidence exists.

## The actual starting product

Rebirth is a small first-party historical narrative game: Aelia investigates conflicting claims over a six-sack cart, can use evidence, money or household service, and faces different allocations, relationships, access and later commissions. These routes execute real accounts, goods and commitments. Six authored NPCs have limited plans and retained information. Saves and alternate histories remain inspectable. This is more specific than an open chat town and much smaller than a city simulator.

The commercial hypothesis is that **a player returns because they care what their agreement did to a person or opportunity**. A changed trust number is insufficient. The first release candidate should make a later closed counter, missed promise or reopened account visible and understandable, then offer another worthwhile decision. Current commissions and relationship repair supply a mechanism; they have not established that people want it.

## Choose one buyer first

| Market | What it must prove before investment |
|---|---|
| Experiential entertainment — first priority | Adult narrative/systemic-game players choose to continue and return; clear onboarding, memorable people, sufficient content and stable saves. |
| Historical education | Expert review, age-appropriate learning goals and measured learning beyond exposure; separate institutional sales and support. Archaeological labels do not establish pedagogy. |
| Professional simulation | A domain-specific validated model, accountable outcomes and a paying workflow; this Pompeii episode is not evidence of fit. |
| Agent research | Stable tasks, reproducible baselines and useful evaluation demand. A research environment is a different product from a game. |
| Creator tools | Outside authors can build and debug worthwhile scenarios with acceptable effort and dependable distribution. Extensible code alone is insufficient. |
| Adaptive learning environments | Individual learners or institutional learning buyers need measured transfer, useful adaptation and an identifiable curriculum/procurement budget; subscription or institution licensing would require separate validation. Cognitive acceleration remains speculative. |
| Future immersive experiences | Consumers, venues or headset-experience buyers would need a compelling embodied experience at viable hardware/content/serving costs; ticketed sessions or consumer sales are untested possibilities. Neither demand nor a cognition benefit is established. |

## A plausible route with explicit gates

First ship a compact, polished episode and run the prospective [six formative plus twelve uncoached sessions](PRODUCT_VALIDATION.md). Keep the offline game available. Repair the dominant observed reasons people stop. Only after that, test unprompted returns with a larger frozen cohort and a second consequential chapter. Do not add a reminder and call its effect organic retention.

Distribution should initially be a playable local/downloadable demo, honest gameplay clips and an opt-in test page aimed at historical narrative and systemic-game communities. These are proposed channels; no outreach or public deployment has occurred. A small paid episode or paid content expansion is a plausible first monetization test. A subscription needs recurring value and demonstrated return behavior. No price or conversion rate has been validated.

Authoring tools follow repeated first-party production: extract the contracts, evidence permissions, opportunity conditions and economic assertions actually reused in a second district. Then test five independent authors on one bounded scenario each, recording time, support required and accounting/provenance failures. Distribution and marketplace work follow usable authoring plus an audience, not before them.

## Moat hypotheses and what would falsify them

The implementation is reproducible and largely copyable: event sourcing, persistent data, grounded action validation and constrained plans are established techniques. A stronger incumbent can add them. A potential advantage would be a growing library of coherent episodes, a reliable authoring/test workflow for interacting obligations and private information, and earned trust that saved choices remain meaningful. None is established yet. Interoperable world definitions could lower switching costs as readily as create defensibility.

Measure content production time, failure recovery and return behavior alongside runtime. If a second polished episode remains expensive to author, or players cannot distinguish its causal persistence from a conventional branching narrative, the platform thesis weakens. If outside authors need the engine developer to repair every scenario, there is no scalable creator product.

## Costs that matter

The present default makes zero external inference calls. A future per-active-hour budget must include dialogue turns, context size, retries, consolidation, narration, speech, hosting, save growth and support; see `AI_COST_MODEL.md`. Meter actual usage before promising a subscription margin. Bound calls at significant decisions, cache only with access-safe state revisions, and keep authoritative economic updates deterministic. The new ledger optimization can lower transaction copying/hashing, but adds write work; it is not evidence of cheap million-agent hosting.

The next funding or expansion decision should rest on recorded voluntary behavior, reproducible product reliability and measured content costs. This sprint can provide a stronger prototype; it cannot provide product-market fit.

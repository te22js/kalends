# Historical additions for the Rebirth dispute

Checked 21 October 2026. The prior
[23-claim ledger](../pompeii/HISTORICAL_SOURCE_LEDGER.md) remains unchanged.
These additions constrain a fictional commercial dispute; they do not turn its
particular people, promises, payments or settlement rules into attested history.

| ID | Supported claim | Evidence and implementation restriction |
|---|---|---|
| RBH24 | Wax writing boards recorded commercial transactions at Pompeii. | The Park guide, printed p51, describes Caecilius Iucundus's archive and payments/commission. This is comparative evidence from another house/district and earlier years, not a recovered tablet belonging to Felix or Marcus. |
| RBH25 | Wax tablets could be folded/sealed; formal documents could involve witnesses. | State Numismatic Portal guide, printed p38. A seal is not proof that every statement is true. A readable receipt in the game has an invented author, date and contents. |
| RBH26 | Goods including cereals could have their capacity checked at the Mensa Ponderaria. | Park guide, printed p85. The actual table belongs in the Forum area, outside the small playable block. A local paid inspection and its price/duration remain synthetic rules. |
| RBH27 | Commercial and domestic spaces could be connected. | Park guide, printed p23, on Vetutius Placidus's thermopolium and rear residence. This does not establish the exact restored roof, furnishings, business identity or inhabitants in the new artwork. |
| RBH28 | The game's flour deposit, priority claim, negotiations, remedy and household guarantee are authored fiction. | No historical source is claimed for the transaction. Present it as private bargaining among fictional people, not a documented court case or a modern consumer-protection procedure. |
| RBH29 | Reconstruction combines attested material setting and invented spatial/artistic choices. | Keep the prior schematic-map disclaimer. Illustration is not archaeological photographic evidence; do not add modern street addresses or imply surveyed travel times. |

Design implications: a dated tablet can support what was recorded earlier while
leaving present availability uncertain. A merchant can have a material reason to
withhold a private record. Household backing can create asymmetric obligation;
it should not act as a universally benevolent friendship reward. These are
plausible fictional design choices, not validated Roman behavioral estimates.

Free women, freedpeople and enslaved people had different constraints. Preserve
the existing fictional status labels and avoid giving an enslaved messenger an
independent merchant's authority merely to make every interaction symmetrical.
Specific duties, freedom of movement and remedies still require specialist review.
Do not infer a character's legal status solely from their occupation.

The first-century setting, exact eruption-day uncertainty, building indexing,
modern house names and attribution cautions in the prior ledger continue to
apply. No institution has reviewed or endorsed the game. A historian has not
independently validated the economic or legal model.
